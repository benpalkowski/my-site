title: "The difference between 'Sierra Club national' and 'Sierra Club chapters'"
date: "2026-06-29"
time: "12:40 PM"
---
Some organizations operate with legally distinct chapters from the national mother ship.

If you listen carefully, Sierra Club often refers to its state and local chapters as though they are legally distinct from the national Sierra Club organization. In reality, though, the chapters are all part of the same legal entity that is Sierra Club.

In my experience, Sierra Club management gives chapters a fair amount of autonomy in terms of how to raise and spend funds. The local chapters get credit for funds raised and have discretion over how resources are spent. That could all change, however, as Sierra Club faces financial pressures or decides to make a policy change.

So, how can a Sierra Club donor ensure that the funds they donate are not going to be used for salaries and administrative expenses, but actually for the programs they support.

The obvious option is simply to donate to organizations with a more focused mission. You wouldn't be alone. In fact, Sierra Club has reportedly lost 60% of its membership since abandonining its focus on conservation and environmental issues in recent years. Many other local environmental organizations have popped up in recent years, providing an alternative vehicle through which to engage in environmental advocacy. 

However, if a true Sierra Club supporter wishes not to cut off their financial support of Sierra Club, but to inject an element of accountability.

Since Sierra Club's chapters are all part of the same legal entity, a local Sierra Club donor may wish to restrict their contribution to be used only be the local chapter or to designate the donation to a particular local project. If a donor porperly restricts the donation, it will have the full force of law, and Sierra Club would be precluded from using the funds at their leisure.

Another option could be to ensure your donation goes to the Sierra Club Foundation, rather than Sierra Club. The former is a legitimate charity (thereby potentially making a tax deduction benefit available to the donor!), whereas the latter operates akin to a political committee.

So, if you direct your donation to just "Sierra Club," rather than "Sierra Club Foundation," your contribution will be ineligible for a tax write-off and Sierra Club can use your donation for political purposes. If you instead donate to Sierra Club Foundation, then your donation could be elitigble for a tax write-off and would limit Sierra Club's use of the funds to permissible (i.e., non-politicla) purposes.
